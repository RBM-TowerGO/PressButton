TASK
Press the elevator down-call button using the physical finger front end indicated
by the WHITE/CREAM ARROW. Observe the target indicator turn on during THIS RUN,
then withdraw and obtain visual withdrawal evidence before reporting completion.
The arrow identifies a physical end, not the sticker center or a robot direction.
Prefer that marked finger; open, single-finger pressing is allowed.

VISUAL SUBGOALS
Choose the current subgoal from the latest images and observed action effects.
For actions that continue the run, state what should improve in the NEXT image.
Stages follow current evidence, not a fixed motion sequence. Positive X requires
the CURRENT wrist overlap check described below; an old approval cannot carry over.

GET A USEFUL VIEW: Seek a view where the button's full pressable face and the marked
physical finger end can be compared. Use HEAD for the panel and hand together,
and WRIST for the local contact area. If framing hides needed objects, change the
view; about +30 degrees absolute head pitch is a look-down reference. Keep an
already useful view. Centering the panel helps visibility, not fingertip alignment.

USE THE HEAD WHEN THE WRIST LOSES THE BUTTON: The wrist camera is behind the
fingers, so the hand hides the button face as it closes in; a lost wrist button
after forward motion usually means occlusion, not that the button disappeared.
The HEAD camera is not blocked by the hand and is the only view that can show the
target light. Therefore: (1) before the first forward press, make sure the HEAD
view shows the button together with the hand; if it does not, lower the head
(absolute pitch, about +30 is a reference, increase it if the measured pitch is
already near it and the button is still absent), while continuing wrist Y/Z
alignment while the wrist view is usable. (2) When the wrist button turns partial,
hidden or unknown, do not withdraw or reacquire reflexively: look at the HEAD
view first to judge contact, whether the target light is on, and the remaining
gap; lower or adjust the head if the head view cannot show them. (3) Withdraw
only for a reason found in the images, and confirm light-on from the HEAD view.
Read CURRENT_HEAD_VIEW_ADVICE each round: it reports the measured head pitch and
which views currently show the button. It is advisory; head-image offsets are not
wrist Y/Z signs and cannot authorize positive X.

IMPROVE ALIGNMENT: When lateral or height deviation is evident, prioritize
improving the button-to-marked-end relationship over merely moving closer.
Until wrist overlap is confirmed, use Y/Z adjustments with X=0; negative-X
withdrawal, head/gripper adjustment and observation remain available.
Use the WRIST Y/Z DIRECTION TABLE below to choose axis signs. Your task during
alignment is to choose correction MAGNITUDES, not to reinvent those directions.
Use COARSE-TO-FINE Y/Z: for clear large deviations with a supported direction,
choose a larger correction, including simultaneous Y/Z when both need adjustment.
Avoid repeated tiny nudges while the error is plainly large. Near the target,
reduce the step size; after crossing or worsening, reassess and refine. Choose
the magnitude from visual scale and recorded effects, not a guessed fixed
pixel-to-millimeter ratio. There is no fixed coarse/fine step or displacement cap.
Name which horizontal/vertical projection error the action should reduce in a
usable view, and check that change afterward. The reference is the marked
physical end, not the image center, sticker center or whole gripper opening.
Use both views to assess the intended contact path; do not force zero image
offset or treat projection overlap as proof of 3D alignment. A cropped button's
visible-box center is not its full center; acknowledge uncertainty or improve
the view instead of treating that box as a precise alignment target.

WRIST Y/Z DIRECTION TABLE — CURRENT FIXED-ORIENTATION SETUP
Reference: the physical finger end identified by the arrow, not the arrow's
printed center. Compare it with the actual pressable button FACE in the WRIST
image. Image x increases rightward and image y downward. For a readable face
box [left, top, right, bottom] and physical tip [u, v], use these rules:
- Button face LEFT of the tip (right < u): choose +Y, robot-left.
  Expected wrist change: button moves RIGHT toward the marked tip.
- Button face RIGHT of the tip (left > u): choose -Y, robot-right.
  Expected wrist change: button moves LEFT toward the marked tip.
- Button face ABOVE the tip (bottom < v): choose +Z, raise the hand.
  Expected wrist change: button moves DOWN toward the marked tip.
- Button face BELOW the tip (top > v): choose -Z, lower the hand.
  Expected wrist change: button moves UP toward the marked tip.
- If the tip already lies inside the face's interval on one image axis,
  use zero correction on that axis; exact center matching is unnecessary.
For example, button LEFT AND ABOVE -> xyz_mm=[0, +a, +b], with a,b positive
millimeter magnitudes you select. LEFT AND BELOW -> [0, +a, -b]. Combine the
two axes when both need correction. Keep X=0 until the wrist overlap check.
CURRENT_WRIST_ALIGNMENT_DIRECTION_RULE applies this table to THIS observation's
location estimates. If the objects match the current wrist image, use its signs
and select nonzero correction magnitudes; do not reinterpret positive as negative.
If the estimated objects are wrong or unidentifiable, explain the mismatch and
reacquire them. A zero XYZ move is not an alignment step or a request for images;
use request_observation when you need another observation.

These are task-specific direction rules supported by recorded pure Y/Z moves,
not a universal camera transform or a pixel-to-millimeter calibration. They
apply to the present wrist mounting, unmirrored images and preserved hand
orientation; do not apply this table to HEAD image offsets. If the setup changes
or reliable actual motion contradicts the expected image change, reacquire the
objects and reassess the mapping instead of guessing an opposite sign. Cropped
faces, changing object identity and drifting tip estimates are not precise targets.

CHECK PROGRESS WITHOUT OSCILLATING
Compare the SAME physical button and finger end before and after each action.
If +Z brought the button down toward the tip and it is STILL ABOVE, continue +Z
with an appropriate magnitude; do not switch to -Z merely because the view was
restored. Reverse the alignment sign only after a visible crossing of the target
interval, or after explicitly resolving contradictory motion evidence. The same
rule applies to Y. Reduce magnitude near overlap or after overshoot.
Do not declare progress solely because error_button_units became smaller: its
denominator is an estimated button-box size that may change. Check the actual
images and pixel offsets of consistent objects; clipping or a changing reference
can invalidate that comparison. Loss of the button is not alignment improvement.

APPROACH / PRESS: Positive X, alone or combined with Y/Z, is allowed only after
an independent check of the CURRENT WRIST original image confirms that the
arrow-marked physical fingertip POINT lies inside the target button FACE BOX in
BOTH horizontal and vertical image coordinates (box boundary included).
Partial/unidentifiable targets or tips and unknown overlap do not pass. This is
a wrist-only 2D precondition, not a head-view test or proof of 3D contact.
When current wrist evidence suggests overlap, propose the desired XYZ; the
program performs this independent check before sending the command.
After it passes, choose XYZ from the evidence without an X step cap. Every new
observation requires a fresh check before another positive-X command. If overlap
is lost, return to Y/Z alignment or improve the view. A rejected command has not
executed and is not clipped; do not repeat positive X on that same rejected image.
You never request or wait for this check: the program runs it automatically when
you propose positive X, and again on every new observation. After a rejection,
you may propose positive X again on a later observation.
Seeing the button or failing to trigger it does not prove only distance remains.

MERGED-END PRESS (only when CURRENT_PRESS_PHASE is supplied): the contact end is
the merged CLOSED gripper, not a single open finger, and the program supplies its
fixed wrist pixel. This overrides the single-finger preference above. Closing the
gripper moves only the fingers, not the button or the camera, so align with the
gripper OPEN onto that fixed pixel, then close, then press.
Outside the close band, once the aim point is inside the button face box, approach
with positive X directly; do not chase the exact center first. Inside the close
band, before ranging and closing: if the aim point sits on the button's edge or
upper rim, fine-tune Y/Z toward the middle of the button face, then range and close.
The button box jitters by a few percent between frames; correct only an offset
clearly larger than that jitter, and do not reverse a small step just because the
box moved slightly.
The wrist camera is mounted ABOVE the gripper, so it views the contact point from
above and there is a parallax between the image and the actual contact path. The
aim pixel is calibrated at contact depth (about 150 mm); when the hand is farther
from the panel, a button shown on the aim pixel lies slightly BELOW the path the
closed end will travel, so the press tends to land high. Account for this
viewpoint difference when aligning, especially in Z, and use the HEAD view to
check the real height of the closed end relative to the button.
ORDER: (1) with the gripper OPEN, finish Y/Z alignment AND the range measurement;
(2) then CLOSE the gripper; (3) then press by the measured distance. The open
gripper is for aiming and ranging only, not for touching the panel or pressing.
The range is measured only when the open-gripper wrist depth at the aim point lies
inside the close band (close_band_mm in CURRENT_PRESS_PHASE); when it is farther,
approach with positive X sized so the next depth reading lands inside that band,
not at or beyond the contact plane (tip_plane_mm). Use the reported depth reading
(this_observation_range) to size that approach step.
HEAD VIEW AS SUPPLEMENT: throughout these stages, use the HEAD view to estimate
how far the gripper still is from the panel, whether it is in front of the target
button and whether it is touching anything; it complements the wrist depth and is
the final confirmation of the target light. Head offsets are not Y/Z signs or
millimeters. Follow the stage:
- align_open: keep the gripper open; Y/Z alignment and approach into the close
  band as described above. Do not close before the range is frozen.
- close: the range is frozen; close with gripper c while the button still sits on
  the aim point. Do not move forward while open.
- press (DIRECT PRESS STAGE, first approach after closing): the closed tips
  usually hide the button in the wrist view, by design. No separate button
  localization, wrist direction rule or coordinate offset is supplied in this stage. Estimate the closed end versus the
  target button yourself from the HEAD and WRIST images together, plus live depth,
  and act directly. Do not withdraw or reopen merely because the wrist button is
  hidden. Approach with positive X, at most remaining_mm; for this first stroke it
  is recomputed from live wrist depth down to approach_stop_depth_mm (contact plus
  a standoff), capped by the frozen range, so the closed end stops in front of the
  panel. A larger stroke is rejected, not clipped. Later strokes are bounded down
  to target_depth_mm (contact plus overpress).
- head_align (after the FIRST closed forward stroke, on the first attempt and
  every retry): the approach stops at the standoff, before contact. Before the
  FINAL press, judge whether the closed end is in front of the target button face:
  HEAD image FIRST (not blocked by the hand), then the WRIST image (at the
  standoff the button edge or ring is often still visible around the closed tips
  and shows the remaining offset at close range). If they disagree, a clear WRIST
  offset beats an uncertain HEAD impression; HEAD decides when the WRIST button is
  hidden. If the button is visible in NEITHER the HEAD nor the WRIST image, the
  verdict must be unclear and positive X is not allowed. Then either withdraw a
  little with negative X until the WRIST view shows the button edge again, or
  adjust the head angle so the HEAD view sees the button past the gripper.
  Every propose_move carries head_alignment {verdict, head_view,
  wrist_view}. aligned (HEAD on the button, WRIST no clear offset) -> press with
  positive X, Y=0, Z=final_press_z_mm: the first final press after each withdrawal
  also moves 10 mm DOWN (Z=-10). This does not stack; further strokes before the
  next withdrawal use Z=0. offset/unclear -> fine-tune Y/Z only (X=0, a few mm;
  withdraw a little first if the end touches the panel) and judge again on the
  next image; several rounds are normal. For WRIST offsets use the WRIST Y/Z
  DIRECTION TABLE (button face relative to the merged end); for HEAD offsets
  image-left is usually robot left (+Y), image-up is +Z. Positive X without verdict aligned is rejected. Re-judge before each further
  stroke. If many rounds do not converge, open the gripper and realign.
- press_reached_withdraw: the closed press reached its target. Stop pushing; the
  next action is fixed: withdraw exactly 10 mm, propose_move xyz_mm=[-10, 0, 0].
- withdrawn_check_light: judge the light in the HEAD view. On -> report_done.
  Not on -> open the gripper (o); the program then localizes the button again.

VERIFY / WITHDRAW: Confirm this run's target light-on, then withdraw and obtain
withdrawal evidence before reporting done. An executed command alone is not progress.
The light must STILL be on in a HEAD image taken AFTER the withdrawal, with the hand
clear of the button. A bright ring seen only while the hand covers the button may
be a reflection and does not count.

MULTIPLE ATTEMPTS: A press can reach the panel without landing on the button.
Once the closed press reaches its target (live depth at target_depth_mm or the
frozen range used up), the program fixes the next steps: (1) withdraw exactly
10 mm with propose_move xyz_mm=[-10, 0, 0], no Y/Z; (2) judge the light in the
HEAD view after that withdrawal: on -> report_done; not on -> it was a MISS, not
too little force, so OPEN the gripper (o). Do not press, shift Y/Z or close again
with the gripper still closed; those are rejected. (3) After opening, the frozen
range is reset and the button is localized again: realign Y/Z with the gripper
open (also use where the closed end landed relative to the button in the HEAD and
WRIST images: left/right, above/below, on the rim), let the range freeze, close,
approach once, repeat the head_align check (fine-tune Y/Z until HEAD, combined
with WRIST, shows aligned) and make the final press by live depth. Push further only to finish an incomplete press,
when remaining_mm is still clearly above zero. Use report_unable only when you see no reasonable further attempt.

EVIDENCE AND DECISION
Use the current HEAD and WRIST images, independent model locations, raw image
offsets, and actual command feedback to choose the next action. Locations are
model estimates, not validated measurements. Compare the observed effect with
the previous action's intended visual subgoal; do not carry old alignment claims
into a changed image. Unknown localization does not prohibit Y/Z alignment,
withdrawal or improving the view, but cannot authorize positive X.
Repeated motion without demonstrated
progress triggers a read-only hypothesis review before the next action decision.
Repeated forward motion also requires this review: approach is not contact proof.
Read MOTION_HYPOTHESIS_REVIEW and provide the required reassessment, including
specific current evidence, expected visual change and what to reconsider if the
change is absent. Merely seeing the button does not establish finger alignment;
failure to light does not by itself mean you need more forward distance.
If a location appears wrong, explain the specific image evidence; choose the next
action from the images and feedback instead of blindly following that estimate.

Canonical coordinates use 0..1, x to the right and y down; pixel_delta uses pixels.
Coordinates with invalid or unspecified units remain unknown. Raw dx/dy are the button
box center minus the marked physical end, computed separately per camera. A box
around a partially visible button describes only the visible part. Do not combine
points from different cameras or assume identity where the marker is unclear.
Image offsets are projections, not millimeter corrections or proof of contact.
Camera motion, gripper shape, and scale may affect their meaning; judge these from
the images and recorded state. The wrist camera moves with the hand. Head motion
changes the head view, not the hand or wrist camera. Choose the expected image
change using the WRIST Y/Z DIRECTION TABLE, then choose the magnitudes from
recorded action effects and visual scale. Do not independently remap its signs
or transfer them to another camera. Existing usable motion evidence can guide
the next action; no fresh probe is required every round.

COMMANDS
Choose one command. move xyz_mm=[X,Y,Z] is an incremental chassis displacement in

+X forward, -X withdraw, +Y robot LEFT, -Y robot RIGHT, +Z up, -Z down. Check that
the robot direction stated in your reason agrees with the numeric signs.
head head_deg=[yaw,pitch] uses absolute degrees.
gripper c closes and o opens; startup preserves the opening. There is no automatic

Decide whether to move forward using current visual evidence and actual feedback.
Invalid depth or an unconfirmed trigger alone does not prohibit forward motion
once the current wrist overlap check passes. Negative X never needs this check.
Fixed-point depth is a distance reference, not calibrated fingertip clearance.
