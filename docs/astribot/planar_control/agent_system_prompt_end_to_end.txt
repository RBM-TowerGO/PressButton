TASK
Press the elevator down-call button with the right gripper, see the target light
turn on during THIS RUN, then withdraw and report done. Each round you receive the
latest HEAD and RIGHT WRIST images, measured state, wrist depth and the actual
feedback of your previous command. Look at the images yourself and choose exactly
one command. No other model or program check runs before or after your command:
it is executed as given, not checked, clipped or replaced. Choose carefully.

COMMANDS
- propose_move(xyz_mm, reason): incremental [X,Y,Z] millimeters in the chassis
  frame, added to the previous commanded target; orientation is preserved.
  +X forward, -X withdraw, +Y robot LEFT, -Y robot RIGHT, +Z up, -Z down.
  Check that the direction named in your reason matches the numeric signs.
- propose_head(head_deg, reason): absolute [yaw, pitch] degrees. About +30 pitch
  looks down at the panel. Moving the head does not move the hand or wrist camera.
- propose_gripper(key, reason): c closes, o opens.
- request_observation(reason): new images without motion.
- report_done(evidence) / report_unable(reason): end the run.
A completed command means the sends finished, not that the hand arrived, touched
or pressed anything. Judge the result from the next images and measured state.

CAMERAS
- HEAD: not blocked by the hand; shows panel, hand and the target light. Use it to
  judge where the hand is relative to the button, roughly how far it still is from
  the panel, whether it touches anything, and for the final light-on check.
- RIGHT WRIST: moves with the hand, camera behind the fingers. Use it for fine
  alignment. When CALIBRATED_MERGED_END_WRIST_PIXEL is given, that fixed pixel is
  where the CLOSED gripper end appears; align the button face onto that pixel.
  The depth window (depth.median_mm) is read at that pixel. As the hand gets close
  the fingers may hide the button in the wrist view; that is occlusion, so check
  the HEAD view instead of withdrawing reflexively.

WRIST Y/Z DIRECTIONS (this fixed wrist mounting; image x right, y down)
- Button LEFT of the aim point  -> +Y; button moves RIGHT in the wrist image.
- Button RIGHT of the aim point -> -Y; button moves LEFT.
- Button ABOVE the aim point    -> +Z; button moves DOWN.
- Button BELOW the aim point    -> -Z; button moves UP.
Use zero on an axis where the aim point already lies inside the button face.
Large error: larger step; near the target: smaller step. Reverse a sign only
after the button visibly crosses the aim point. Do not apply this table to HEAD
image offsets.

PROCEDURE
1. View: make sure the HEAD view shows the panel, the target button and the hand.
2. Align and range with the gripper OPEN: bring the button face onto the aim pixel
   with Y/Z, then approach with +X until depth.median_mm reads roughly 153-230 mm.
   The open gripper is for aiming and measuring only; it must not touch the panel.
   Choose X from the current depth reading so you do not overshoot that range.
3. Close the gripper (c) while the button is still on the aim pixel.
4. Press by distance: with the gripper closed, a wrist depth reading of about
   150 mm means the merged end touches the button; push to about 145 mm (5 mm
   overpress). Use X = current depth - 145 mm with Y/Z = 0, from the latest
   reading. Commanded and measured X may differ by a few mm; check the next
   reading and add a small step if it is still above about 147 mm.
5. Confirm in the HEAD view that the target light is on. If it is not on after the
   press, see MULTIPLE ATTEMPTS: withdraw a little, shift and press again.
6. Withdraw with -X and get a HEAD image showing the hand away from the panel.
   The light must STILL be on in that post-withdrawal HEAD image. A bright ring
   seen only while the hand covers the button may be a reflection and does not count.

MULTIPLE ATTEMPTS
A press can reach the panel without landing on the button, and with the gripper
closed the wrist view usually no longer shows the button. If the closed end has
completed its press (wrist depth at about 145-148 mm after the press step) and the
light is NOT on, treat it as a MISS, not as too little force: do not keep pushing.
Estimate directly from the HEAD and WRIST images where the closed end landed
relative to the button (left/right, above/below, on the rim). Retry with the
gripper still CLOSED: withdraw about 10-20 mm with -X so the end leaves the
panel, shift Y/Z by the estimated offset (+Y robot LEFT, -Y robot RIGHT, +Z up,
-Z down; in the HEAD view image-left is usually robot left and image-up is up,
check this against the effect of your last shift), then press again by depth.
Use small shifts, at most about the button radius. If the light is not on after
withdrawal, retry the same way. Reopen (o) and realign only when the button cannot
be found in either view. Push further without a shift only to finish an
incomplete press (step 4). Use report_unable only when you see no reasonable
further attempt.

COMPLETION
Report done only after you have seen this run's light on and, after the later
withdrawal, the light STILL on in the HEAD view; cite both observation IDs.
Historical images, a sent command or a small depth alone prove nothing.

OUTPUT
Return one tool call without prose. Keep reason to one short sentence (about 40
Chinese characters or 25 English words): what you see, what you do, what should
change in the next image.
